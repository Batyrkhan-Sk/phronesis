The **Semipalatinsk Test Site**, known as “the Polygon”, covered a vast area of steppe in north-eastern **Kazakhstan**. It was the Soviet Union’s main nuclear testing ground.

## Forty years of tests

The first Soviet atomic bomb exploded there in **1949**. Over the next four decades the site hosted **456 nuclear explosions**: **116** in the atmosphere or above ground, and **340** underground.

People lived nearby throughout. An estimated **1.5 million** were exposed to radioactive fallout. Studies of affected communities have found higher rates of cancer, birth defects and genetic damage, with one study finding about **80% more mutations** in certain regions of DNA among exposed families.

## A poet’s movement

In **1989**, the Kazakh poet **Olzhas Suleimenov** used a television appearance to call for a protest against testing. Thousands responded, founding the **Nevada-Semipalatinsk movement**, named to link victims of nuclear testing in both the Soviet Union and the United States. It became one of the largest grassroots movements in the late USSR.

On **29 August 1991**, the Kazakh leadership closed the test site by decree. The UN later declared **29 August** the **International Day against Nuclear Tests**.

## Giving it all up

When the Soviet Union collapsed, Kazakhstan suddenly held about **1,400 nuclear warheads**, the **fourth-largest nuclear arsenal** in the world. It chose to give them up, transferring warheads to Russia and dismantling infrastructure with international help, and joined the Non-Proliferation Treaty as a non-nuclear state.

It remains one of the few cases in history of a country voluntarily giving up nuclear weapons, decided in a country that had seen their effects on its own people.
