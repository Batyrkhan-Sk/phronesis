The **California Coastal Records Project** photographed the entire California coastline from a helicopter to document erosion: more than 12,000 images. One of them, image #3850, happened to include Barbra Streisand’s clifftop home.

She sued the photographer, Kenneth Adelman, for **$50 million** over invasion of privacy. Before the lawsuit, the image had been **downloaded six times**, two of those by her own lawyers. Once the lawsuit was reported, **more than 400,000 people** visited the site in the following month.

The case was **dismissed**, and Streisand was ordered to pay Adelman’s legal fees.

## Naming it

In 2005, **Mike Masnick** of the blog *Techdirt* coined the term “Streisand effect” for a different case of a takedown backfiring. It stuck because the pattern keeps recurring: companies suing critics, governments blocking websites, celebrities trying to remove unflattering photos.

## Why it happens

- Suppression is **news**. A lawsuit is a better story than the photo.
- Forbidden information becomes **more desirable** (psychologists call this reactance).
- Online, copying is effortless, so every takedown attempt invites copies.
