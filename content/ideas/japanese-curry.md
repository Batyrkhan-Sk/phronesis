**Japanese curry**, thick, mild and brown, poured over rice with chunks of potato and carrot, is nothing like most Indian curries. That’s because it didn’t come directly from India.

## Via the Royal Navy

During the **Meiji era**, as Japan rapidly modernised, curry appeared in Japanese cookbooks by **1872**. It most likely came through the **British**, whose Royal Navy cooks used ready-made **curry powder**, itself a British invention for imitating Indian flavours. The British style used a flour-thickened sauce, a bit like a stew.

## Curry against beriberi

The **Imperial Japanese Navy** had a serious problem: sailors living mainly on **white rice** were getting **beriberi**, a disease caused by lack of **thiamine** (vitamin B1). Curry with meat and vegetables over rice was quick to make and helped prevent it.

Sailors went home and took curry with them. Today Japan’s **Maritime Self-Defense Force** still serves curry **every Friday**, and each ship’s galley has its own recipe.

## The apple twist

People often ask why Japanese curry roux contains **apple and honey**. The answer is a 1963 product: **House Foods’ “Vermont Curry”**, made with apples and honey to appeal to children. It was a huge success, and sweet, mild curry became a childhood staple. By **2000**, curry was eaten more often in Japan than **sushi or tempura**.
