## The legend

In June 1889, the story goes, King Umberto I and **Queen Margherita of Savoy** were staying in Naples. The pizzaiolo **Raffaele Esposito** was summoned and made three pizzas. The queen preferred the one in the colours of the new **Italian flag**: red tomato, white mozzarella and green basil. Esposito named it after her, and a letter from the royal household thanking him still hangs in the pizzeria.

It’s a perfect story about a new nation, a popular queen and a humble dish.

## The problems

- **No newspaper** of the time reported the royal visit to a pizzeria.
- Researchers have **questioned the authenticity** of the thank-you letter.
- The story, and the name *Margherita*, were first **widely promoted in the 1930s and 1940s**.
- Neapolitan writers described pizzas with **tomato, mozzarella and basil** long before 1889: Emanuele Rocco in **1849** and Francesco De Bourcard’s collection in **1866**.

## What’s probably true

Tomato, mozzarella and basil was already a standard Neapolitan combination. The royal connection seems to be a later invention that made it famous, and it made it a national symbol.

**A useful takeaway:** when a food’s origin story involves royalty and a precise date, be suspicious. The croissant and the Ottoman siege of Vienna is another example.
