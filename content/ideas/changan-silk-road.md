**Xi’an** means “Western Peace”, a name given in the Ming dynasty. For most of its history, the city was called **Chang’an**: “Perpetual Peace”.

## A capital for a thousand years

Chang’an was the capital of several dynasties, including the **Western Han** and the **Tang**, between **202 BC and 907 AD**. Chinese tradition often counts **thirteen** dynasties that ruled from the area, depending on how you count.

## A million people

Around **750**, Chinese records called Tang Chang’an a “**million-man city**”. Modern estimates put the population inside its walls at **800,000 to 1,000,000**, with perhaps two million in the wider area. Along with **Constantinople** and **Baghdad**, it was one of the largest cities in the world.

The Tang city was laid out on a strict **grid** of walled wards, with two great markets:

- the **East Market**, for Chinese goods and crafts;
- the **West Market**, the Silk Road bazaar, where Persian and Central Asian merchants sold horses, jewels, glass and spices.

## A cosmopolitan capital

At the eastern end of the overland **Silk Road**, Chang’an drew **Sogdians, Persians, Arabs, Indians** and monks of the **Church of the East**, Christians who reached China in the 7th century. Buddhism, Zoroastrianism, Manichaeism, Christianity and Islam all had communities there.

Many of them stayed. Their descendants are part of why Xi’an still has a large **Muslim Quarter** today.
