Before the telegraph, information could travel only as fast as a horse, a ship or a pigeon. After it, news crossed oceans in minutes. The first transatlantic cable briefly worked in 1858, and a lasting one opened in 1866. Tom Standage calls it **“the Victorian internet”**, and the comparison holds up well.

- **Slang and shorthand.** Operators paid by the word, or just bored, invented abbreviations, much like texting. “OK” was one of their favourites.
- **Online romance.** Operators chatted over idle lines, flirted, and occasionally married. Novels such as *Wired Love* (1880) were written about it.
- **Hackers and scams.** Swindlers intercepted horse-race results or stock prices and placed bets before the news arrived.
- **Information overload.** Newspapers complained that the flood of instant news made people shallow and anxious.
- **Encryption.** Businesses bought codebooks that compressed sentences into single words, partly for privacy and partly because telegrams were charged by the word.

Each new communication technology tends to bring back the same social patterns: speed, intimacy, fraud and anxiety. Often only the hardware is new.
