The log entry for the afternoon of 9 September 1947 at Harvard reads: **“Relay #70 Panel F (moth) in relay. First actual case of bug being found.”** Next to it, still taped to the page, is the moth.

The word “actual” is the giveaway: the joke only works if “bug” already meant a fault. It did. **Thomas Edison** wrote about “bugs” in his inventions in letters from 1878, and the term was common engineering slang by the early 20th century.

## Grace Hopper’s role

**Grace Hopper**, a pioneering programmer on the Mark computers, loved telling this story, and it became attached to her. She did not find the moth (the team’s engineers did), and she did not coin the word. She **made the story famous**, and she was a remarkable figure anyway: she was a driving force behind COBOL and the idea that programs could be written in something close to English.

The logbook page is now in the Smithsonian. It may be the only famous software bug you can look at in person.
