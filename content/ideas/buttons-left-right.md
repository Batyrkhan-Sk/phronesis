Look at a man’s shirt and a woman’s shirt. On the man’s, the buttons sit on the **right** and the holes on the left. On the woman’s, it is the other way round. Almost everyone has noticed, and almost everyone has been told a reason.

## The usual explanations

- **Maids.** Wealthy women were dressed by servants, who faced them. Buttons on the wearer’s left were easier for a right-handed maid to fasten. This is the one most often repeated.
- **Swords.** A man wore his sword on the left and drew it with the right hand, so the coat opened with the left hand and the right stayed free.
- **Babies.** A mother holding a baby in the left arm can then undo her clothes with the right hand.
- **Riding.** Some versions say the arrangement stopped wind blowing a rider’s clothes open.
- **Napoleon.** Some retellings say he started it, either to hide his hand in his coat or to stop women dressing like soldiers. I found nothing that would support this, so treat it as a legend.

## Why none of them settles it

Each story explains *half* the puzzle. The sword theory covers men but not women. The maid theory covers women but not men, and it runs into a **timeline problem**: servants dressed rich women for centuries, but this style of buttoning on women’s clothes only becomes common in the 19th century.

The Victorian sexologist **Havelock Ellis** offered a more pointed reading in 1894, that the difference was meant to suggest women were inferior. That, too, is a guess about motives, and is best repeated as one.

## What is safe to say

The convention is real and quite recent in its modern form. Several plausible reasons are consistent with it. Nobody has shown which one, if any, is the cause.

That makes it a good dinner table subject: it is the rare question where the best answer is “we don’t know”, and where you can add that every confident explanation is a story.
