In 1884 the yacht **Mignonette** was being sailed from England to Australia when she sank in the South Atlantic. Four men got away in a small dinghy with almost no food or water: **Tom Dudley**, the captain, **Edwin Stephens**, **Edmund Brooks**, and the cabin boy, **Richard Parker**, who was 17.

## What happened

After about twenty days adrift, Dudley and Stephens killed Parker, who was weakest, and the survivors ate. Brooks did not take part. The survivors were rescued, and Dudley and Stephens were put on trial.

## What the court decided

In **R v Dudley and Stephens** the judges had to answer a question that is still a classic of law teaching: can **necessity**, being desperate to survive, justify deliberately killing an innocent person? The court said **no**. Its reasoning was that the law cannot treat one life as worth less than another, and that a rule allowing the strongest to decide who must die would be dangerous.

The two men were sentenced to death, with a recommendation of mercy, and the sentence was then **commuted to six months in prison**. Brooks was not charged and became a witness.

## The name

Here is the part to bring to dinner. In **Edgar Allan Poe’s** 1838 novel *The Narrative of Arthur Gordon Pym of Nantucket*, shipwrecked sailors draw lots to decide who will be killed and eaten, and the person chosen is called **Richard Parker**. That is 46 years before the real Richard Parker died.

Almost certainly it is a **coincidence**: no one has shown that the *Mignonette* crew knew the novel or chose the name. The author Yann Martel is reported to have taken the name of the tiger in *Life of Pi* from these stories.

## Why law students still read it

The decision is a good example of a court refusing a tempting exception. Necessity can still matter in other kinds of case, and in this one the judges accepted that the men’s plight was awful. What they would not do was make it a legal defence to murder. The question of whether they should have been punished at all is one that people honestly disagree on.
