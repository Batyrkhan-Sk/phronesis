**Leonardo of Pisa** grew up partly in North Africa, where his father worked as a customs official. There he learned the numerals used by Arab mathematicians, which had come from India: nine digits, **a zero**, and **place value**.

To a European merchant using Roman numerals and counting boards, this was transformative. Try multiplying **MCMXLIV by LXXVIII** on paper. Then try 1944 × 78.

His *Liber Abaci* (Book of Calculation, 1202) was a practical handbook: currency conversion, interest, profit shares, weights and measures, all worked through with the new numerals. It helped spread them through Italian commerce, and from there to the rest of Europe.

## About those rabbits

One exercise asks how many pairs of rabbits there will be after a year if each pair produces a new pair every month from its second month. The answer unfolds as **1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21…**, with each number the sum of the previous two.

It was a minor puzzle in a big book. The sequence itself had already appeared in **Indian prosody**, in counting the rhythmic patterns of Sanskrit poetry, centuries earlier. The name “Fibonacci” was attached to it in the 1800s.
