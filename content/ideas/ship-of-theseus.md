According to **Plutarch**, the Athenians kept the ship of their legendary hero **Theseus** for centuries, replacing rotting planks with new timber. Philosophers argued about it: was it still Theseus’s ship?

In 1655, **Thomas Hobbes** made it harder. Suppose someone collected all the discarded planks and **rebuilt the original ship** from them. Now there are two ships. Which is the real one?

## Possible answers

- **Continuity:** the ship that has been continuously sailed and repaired is the original.
- **Material:** the one made from the original planks is the original.
- **Function and name:** identity is a label we assign for practical purposes, and there is no deeper fact.
- **Four-dimensional view:** objects are stretched through time, so “the ship” is the whole history, not a snapshot.

## Why it keeps coming back

- Your body replaces most of its cells over the years (though not all; many neurons last a lifetime).
- Bands with no original members still tour under the original name.
- A codebase rewritten line by line still ships under the same version history.
- Cities are rebuilt on the same sites for centuries.

The puzzle doesn’t have one correct answer. It shows that “same” has **several meanings**, and that many arguments are really disagreements about which one people mean.
