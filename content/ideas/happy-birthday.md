The melody started life as **“Good Morning to All”**, written by the Kentucky schoolteacher **Mildred Hill** and her sister **Patty**, a pioneer of kindergarten education, and published in 1893 in a book of songs for children. At some point people started singing “Happy birthday to you” to the same tune.

## The copyright

A copyright for the birthday lyrics was registered in **1935**. Through a chain of acquisitions it ended up with **Warner/Chappell Music**, which bought the company holding it in 1988. Warner/Chappell charged for public performances in films, TV and adverts, earning an estimated **$2 million a year**. That is why many films had characters sing something else, or cut away.

## The case

In 2013 the filmmaker **Jennifer Nelson**, who was making a documentary *about the song*, was charged $1,500 to use it. Her company and others sued. In **September 2015**, Judge **George H. King** ruled that Warner/Chappell had **never had a valid copyright** to the lyrics. The 1935 registration covered only specific piano arrangements.

Warner/Chappell settled in 2016 for **$14 million**, which went back to people who had paid licence fees. In the United States the song is now treated as being in the **public domain**, and in the EU its copyright expired at the end of 2016.
