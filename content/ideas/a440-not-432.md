When an orchestra tunes before a concert, everyone matches one note: the **A above middle C**. Today it is almost always **440 Hz**, meaning the air vibrates 440 times a second. That number feels like a fact of nature. It is a decision.

## A moving target

For centuries there was no single pitch. A town’s organ, a court’s orchestra and a maker’s tuning fork could all differ. Surviving forks and instruments suggest that pitch in Handel’s era was **lower than today’s**, roughly in the low 420s Hz, though figures for individual forks vary between sources. Over the nineteenth century pitch tended to creep up, because brighter sound impresses in a big hall, and that made singing harder.

France stepped in with a law in **1859**, fixing A at **435 Hz**. Other places kept their own standards for decades, so travelling musicians had to retune.

## 1939: a round number

In **1939** delegates met in London at a conference organised by the International Federation of National Standardizing Associations, and endorsed **A = 440 Hz**. The International Organization for Standardization adopted it in **1955**, and formalised it as **ISO 16** in **1975**. Why 440? Accounts differ: it is a round compromise between the older high and low pitches, and one story adds that it was easier to generate electronically. Either way, it was a practical agreement, not a discovery.

## The 432 Hz myth

You may meet the claim that 440 Hz was forced on the world by the Nazis, or by shadowy industrialists, to unsettle people, and that **432 Hz** is the “natural” or “healing” tuning. Fact-checkers have found no support for this. The 1939 meeting was a standards conference, and nothing in it ties the decision to a plot. Sources trace the modern 432 Hz campaign to the political activist Lyndon LaRouche and the Schiller Institute, which began campaigning in 1986.

Listening to music tuned to 432 Hz is harmless, and some people simply prefer how it sounds. The error is in the claim that it is secretly better for you.

## A kind way to say it

If a friend mentions 432 Hz, you do not need to argue. You can say that pitch has always been a negotiation, and that early-music groups still play lower than 440 on purpose, for the sound of the period.
