**Zoroastrianism** takes its name from the prophet **Zarathustra** (in Greek, Zoroaster). Scholars disagree about when he lived; estimates range across centuries, possibly back to the **2nd millennium BC**. The religion was later the faith of great Persian empires.

## Core ideas

- One supreme wise god, **Ahura Mazda**.
- A struggle between **good and evil**, in which humans choose sides.
- A simple ethical motto: **good thoughts, good words, good deeds**.
- **Fire** as a symbol of purity, kept burning in **fire temples**.

## A shrinking community

After the **Muslim conquest of Persia** in the 7th century, Zoroastrians were gradually reduced to a minority. Many migrated to **western India**, where they became known as **Parsis** (“Persians”). Today estimates of followers worldwide start at about **110,000–120,000**, with the largest groups in **India**, **Iran** and **North America**. Parsis include the **Tata** business family and, most famously, **Freddie Mercury**, born **Farrokh Bulsara** in Zanzibar to Parsi parents.

## A big influence?

Ideas found in Zoroastrian texts, including a **final judgement**, the **resurrection** of the dead, **heaven and hell**, and a coming **saviour**, resemble ideas in Judaism, Christianity and Islam. Some scholars think Zoroastrianism influenced them, especially during the Persian period of Jewish history. Others think the influence is overstated, or ran in several directions. It’s one of the most interesting open questions in the history of religion.
