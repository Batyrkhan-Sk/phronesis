Ask which country has the most time zones and people usually guess **Russia** (11) or the **United States**. The answer is **France**, with **12**, or **13** if you count its claim in Antarctica.

## Why France?

Because of **Overseas France**: territories that are part of the French Republic or attached to it, spread across the globe.

- the Caribbean: **Guadeloupe**, **Martinique**
- South America: **French Guiana**
- the Indian Ocean: **Réunion**, **Mayotte**
- the Pacific: **New Caledonia**, **French Polynesia**, **Wallis and Futuna**
- the North Atlantic: **Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon**

Each uses a different time zone from Paris. When it’s noon in Paris it is already late evening in Nouméa.

## Mainland France runs early

Look at a map: most of **metropolitan France** lies at about the same longitude as Britain. Geographically, it belongs in **GMT**. But France uses **Central European Time**, one hour ahead.

The reason is **1940**. German authorities switched occupied France to German time, and after liberation France **never switched back**. So French winters effectively run on permanent “daylight saving time”, which helps explain the late sunrises and long summer evenings in western France.
