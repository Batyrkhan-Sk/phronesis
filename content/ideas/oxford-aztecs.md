**Oxford** has no exact founding date, but there was teaching there by **1096**. It grew quickly after **1167**, when Henry II banned English students from attending the University of Paris.

The **Mexica** people founded **Tenochtitlan** on an island in Lake Texcoco in **1325** (the traditional date). The empire we call “Aztec”, a **Triple Alliance** of Tenochtitlan, Texcoco and Tlacopan, formed in **1428**, and the Spanish conquest came in 1521.

So Oxford had been teaching for **about 230 years** before Tenochtitlan existed, and more than **330 years** before the Aztec Empire.

## Why it feels wrong

We file the Aztecs under “ancient civilisations” with Egypt and Rome, partly because their world was destroyed so suddenly and completely. Universities feel modern because they still exist. **Survival makes things seem newer, and destruction makes them seem older.**

Tenochtitlan was larger than any city in Spain when the conquistadors arrived, with an estimated 200,000 inhabitants, canals and causeways, and floating gardens. Much of modern Mexico City is built directly on top of it.
