“**We** should go to the cinema.” Does that include you? In English, you have to guess from context.

In many languages you don’t. They have two words:

| Language | Inclusive “we” (you and I) | Exclusive “we” (not you) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Malay / Indonesian | kita | kami |
| Tok Pisin | yumi | mipela |
| Mandarin (northern colloquial) | zánmen | wǒmen |

The Tok Pisin pair says it plainly: *yumi* is “you-me”, *mipela* is roughly “me-fellows”.

## Where it’s found

Linguists call this **clusivity**. It is nearly universal among **Austronesian languages**, from Madagascar to Hawaii, and common in **northern Australia**, **India**, **eastern Siberia**, many **indigenous languages of the Americas**, and parts of **Africa** and the **Caucasus**. No European language outside the Caucasus has it.

## Why it’s worth knowing

It is a good reminder that languages differ in **what they make you say**, not only in what they let you say. A Malay speaker *must* decide whether the listener is included. An English speaker can stay vague, sometimes usefully, as in a manager’s “we need to work harder”.
