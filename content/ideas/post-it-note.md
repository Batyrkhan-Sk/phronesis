In **1968**, **Spencer Silver**, a chemist at 3M, was trying to develop a very strong adhesive for aircraft construction. He ended up with the opposite: tiny **microspheres** of glue that stuck lightly, peeled off cleanly and **could be reused**.

For years Silver gave internal seminars about his “solution without a problem”. Nobody had a use for it.

## The hymn book

**Art Fry**, a 3M product engineer, sang in his church choir and was annoyed that scraps of paper marking his hymns kept **falling out**. In **1974** he remembered Silver’s adhesive and realised it would make the perfect bookmark: sticky enough to stay, gentle enough not to tear the pages.

## The slow launch

The first market test in 1977, under the name **“Press ’n Peel”**, flopped because people didn’t understand the product until they used it. 3M then **handed out free samples** in Boise, Idaho, and most people who tried them wanted to buy more. The national US launch came in **1980**.

The famous **canary yellow** is said to have come from the scrap paper available in a neighbouring lab. Whether or not that is true, it became the product’s signature.

**A useful takeaway:** some of the best inventions are solutions waiting for the right problem, and the right person to notice it.
