Wild carrots have thin, pale, woody roots. People in **Central Asia**, probably around present-day Afghanistan and Iran, domesticated them about a thousand years ago, and the early varieties were **purple and yellow**.

Orange carrots, rich in **beta-carotene**, became widespread through cultivation in the **Netherlands** in the 16th and 17th centuries. They were sweeter, kept well, and spread across Europe.

## The Orange myth

A popular story says Dutch growers bred orange carrots as a **patriotic tribute to William of Orange**, who led the Dutch revolt. It is a lovely idea, and **there is no contemporary evidence** for it. Orange varieties may simply have been better suited to the climate and to people’s tastes.

## The night-vision story

In the Second World War, Britain promoted carrots as helping you **see in the blackout**, and the press credited the night-fighter pilot **John Cunningham**’s success to eating carrots. Many accounts say the story helped **hide the real reason**: new airborne radar. How deliberate the cover story was is debated. What is certain is that vitamin A prevents night blindness in deficient people, and that eating extra carrots will not give anyone superhuman night vision.
