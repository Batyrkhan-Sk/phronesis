**Kiribati** (pronounced “**KIRR-i-bass**”) is a nation of 33 coral atolls and islands scattered across a stretch of the Pacific wider than the continental United States. It sits on the **equator** and across the **180th meridian**, the line the date line roughly follows.

## A country on two different days

Before 1995 the International Date Line ran **through** Kiribati. The capital on **Tarawa** in the west and the **Line Islands** in the east were a **full day apart**. When it was Monday in one part of the country it was Sunday in the other, which left only four shared working days a week for government offices and businesses.

## Moving the line

There is no treaty that fixes the date line. Each country chooses its own time zone. So in **1995** Kiribati shifted its eastern islands across, putting the whole country on the same side. The date line now makes a large **eastward bulge** around Kiribati.

The Line Islands ended up on **UTC+14**, the **earliest time zone on Earth**. It’s 14 hours ahead of London, and 26 hours ahead of the uninhabited US Baker Island, also in the central Pacific. Kiribati promoted itself as the first country to see the dawn of the year **2000**.

## The next problem

Most of Kiribati is only a few metres above sea level. In **2014** the government bought about **20 km²** of land on Vanua Levu in **Fiji**, calling it “an absolute necessity” in case rising seas make parts of the country unlivable.
