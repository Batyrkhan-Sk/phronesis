In **April 1815** Mount Tambora, on the island of Sumbawa in present-day Indonesia, exploded with a force far greater than Krakatoa. Tens of thousands of people died nearby. The eruption threw sulphur high into the stratosphere, where it formed a haze that **cooled the planet**.

The next year, **1816**, became the **“Year Without a Summer”**: snow in New England in June, failed harvests and food riots in Europe.

## A wet week by Lake Geneva

In June 1816, **Lord Byron** had rented the **Villa Diodati** near Geneva. With him were his doctor **John Polidori**, and nearby were **Percy Shelley**, 18-year-old **Mary Godwin** (soon Mary Shelley) and her stepsister **Claire Clairmont**. Relentless storms kept them indoors. They read German ghost stories, and Byron proposed that **each of them write one**.

- Mary, after a sleepless night, imagined a student kneeling beside “the thing he had put together”. That became **Frankenstein** (1818), often called the first science-fiction novel.
- Polidori developed one of Byron’s fragments into **The Vampyre** (1819), the first modern vampire story in English and an ancestor of *Dracula*.
- Byron wrote the bleak poem **“Darkness”**, describing a world in which the sun has gone out.

## Other ripples

- **Painting:** studies of artworks from the following years, including some by J. M. W. Turner, link their **vivid orange skies** to volcanic aerosols, though artistic choice obviously plays a part.
- **The bicycle:** one theory is that the death of horses from lack of feed prompted **Karl Drais** to invent his *Laufmaschine* (1817), a forerunner of the bicycle. It is an attractive story but disputed; Drais never said so himself.
- **Migration:** crop failures pushed many New England farmers west, helping to settle the American Midwest.

The case shows how **climate, chance and culture are connected**: a geological event on one side of the world shaped the imagination of the other.
