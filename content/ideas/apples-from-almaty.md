In the foothills of the **Tian Shan** mountains, around **Almaty** and the Ili valley, there are forests of wild apple trees. Their fruit varies enormously from tree to tree: some small and sour, some large and sweet, in yellow, green and red.

These trees are ***Malus sieversii***, and genetic studies, including the **2010 sequencing of the apple genome**, identified them as the **primary ancestor of the domestic apple**. Your Golden Delicious, Granny Smith or Fuji ultimately descends from trees like these.

## How apples left home

Apples don’t grow true from seed: every seed produces a different tree. Over thousands of years, people and animals moving along the routes that became the **Silk Road** carried the best fruit west and east. Along the way they hybridised with other wild apples, and growers learned to **graft**, copying good trees exactly. The result is the thousands of varieties we know.

## The name of the city

The old Soviet name of Almaty was **Alma-Ata**, often translated as “**father of apples**”, and *alma* means apple in Kazakh. The modern name is commonly explained as “full of apples”. The exact etymology is debated, but the connection to apples is how most locals tell it, and the city celebrates it.

## A forest worth saving

The Russian botanist **Nikolai Vavilov** visited the region in 1929 and recognised it as a centre of origin for apples. Since then many of the wild forests have been lost to farming, building and grazing, and the IUCN lists the species as **vulnerable**. Plant breeders value them as a gene bank: wild trees may hold resistance to diseases that threaten commercial orchards.
