Sunlight contains every colour. When it passes through air, light **scatters off gas molecules** far smaller than its wavelength. Lord Rayleigh showed in the 1870s that this scattering depends very strongly on wavelength: proportional to **1/λ⁴**. Blue light (about 450 nm) scatters roughly **four to five times more** than red (about 700 nm).

So wherever you look away from the Sun, you see **scattered short-wavelength light**: the blue sky.

## Then why not violet?

Violet light scatters even more than blue. Three things work against it:

1. **Sunlight contains less violet** than blue to begin with.
2. Some violet and ultraviolet is **absorbed high in the atmosphere**.
3. Our eyes’ colour sensors are **much less sensitive to violet**, and the mix of light reaching them registers as sky blue.

## Sunsets and Mars

At sunset, light passes through **much more atmosphere**. Most blue is scattered away before it reaches you, leaving **oranges and reds**. Dust, smoke and volcanic particles make sunsets more intense.

On **Mars**, fine dust scatters light differently: the daytime sky looks butterscotch, and sunsets have a **blue glow** around the Sun. They are the reverse of ours.
