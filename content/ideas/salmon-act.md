Section 32 of the **Salmon Act 1986** really is titled **“Handling salmon in suspicious circumstances.”** It makes it an offence to receive or handle salmon (and some related fish) when you **believe, or it would be reasonable to suspect**, that they were taken illegally.

## Why it exists

Wild salmon are valuable and protected. **Poaching** is lucrative, and the hard part of enforcement is the sale, not the catch. The law follows the logic of **handling stolen goods**: you can’t prosecute only the thief, you also need to deter the people buying.

## How to spot a fake law

Lists of “weird laws” go viral constantly (*it’s illegal to die in Parliament*, *you can’t own one guinea pig in Switzerland*). Some are real, many are **exaggerated, misread or invented**. Some quick checks:

- **Is there an official text?** Real UK laws are on *legislation.gov.uk*.
- **What problem was it solving?** Odd-sounding laws usually have a dull, sensible reason.
- **Is it still in force,** or was it repealed long ago?

(The guinea pig one is roughly real: Swiss animal-welfare rules require social animals like guinea pigs to have company.)
