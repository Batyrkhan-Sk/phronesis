## Three hearts

Two **branchial hearts** push blood through the gills. A third, **systemic heart** sends it around the body. When an octopus swims, the systemic heart **stops beating**. That may be one reason octopuses usually crawl: swimming is exhausting.

## Blue blood

Our blood carries oxygen with iron-based haemoglobin. Octopus blood uses **hemocyanin**, which is built around **copper** and turns blue when oxygenated. It works better in cold, low-oxygen water.

## A brain spread through the body

An octopus has around 500 million neurons, and roughly **two-thirds are in its arms**. Each arm can taste, touch and make simple decisions on its own. The philosopher Peter Godfrey-Smith suggests that an octopus is the closest we may come to meeting an **intelligent alien**: its last common ancestor with us was a worm-like creature more than 500 million years ago.

## About the plural

“Octopus” comes from **Greek** (*oktṓpous*, “eight-foot”), not Latin, so the Latin-style plural “octopi” is a mistake. The pedantic Greek plural is “octopodes”. The ordinary English plural, **octopuses**, is the best choice.
