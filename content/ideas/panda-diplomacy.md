Giant pandas live in the wild in only one country: **China**, in the mountain bamboo forests of Sichuan, Shaanxi and Gansu. That makes the animal something no other nation can grow for itself, and China has used it accordingly.

## From gifts…

After **Richard Nixon’s** 1972 visit, China sent the US two pandas, **Ling-Ling and Hsing-Hsing**. Between **1957 and 1983**, **24 pandas** were given to **9 countries**, often marking a new or improving relationship.

## …to rent

In **1984**, under Deng Xiaoping, the policy changed: pandas would be **leased**, not given. The first lease sent two pandas to **Los Angeles** for the Olympic Games at **$50,000 a month each**. Today a standard agreement:

- lasts about **ten years**;
- costs **up to US$1 million a year**, officially used for conservation;
- states that **any cubs born abroad belong to China** and must eventually be sent there.

So when a cub is born in a foreign zoo, it’s celebrated locally, and a few years later it “returns” to a country it has never been to.

## A diplomatic thermometer

Because loans are negotiated government to government, observers read them as signals. When the US National Zoo returned its three pandas in **November 2023**, it was widely reported as a sign of cooler relations, and new loans announced later were read as a thaw. That reading is an interpretation, but a common one.
