**Sikhism** was founded by **Guru Nanak** in Punjab around 1500. One of its earliest institutions is the **langar**, the community kitchen, and it’s still one of the most striking things a visitor can experience.

## Everyone on the floor

The rule is simple: **anyone may eat, and everyone eats the same food, sitting together on the floor**. In a society divided by **caste**, where people of different castes often wouldn’t share food, this was a radical, practical statement of equality. The meal is simple and **vegetarian** (lacto-vegetarian), so that no one’s dietary rules exclude them.

The third Guru, **Guru Amar Das**, reportedly required every visitor, including the Mughal emperor **Akbar**, according to tradition, to eat in the langar before meeting him.

## The Golden Temple

At the **Harmandir Sahib** (Golden Temple) in **Amritsar**, the langar runs around the clock. Reports put the number of people fed at around **50,000 to 100,000 a day**, rising on festivals. Huge cauldrons of dal, flatbreads made by rotating teams and dishwashing lines are run largely by **volunteers** doing *seva*, selfless service.

## Beyond Amritsar

Almost every **gurdwara** in the world runs a langar, and many are open to anyone who walks in. During the COVID-19 pandemic, gurdwaras around the world turned their kitchens into food-distribution centres. One in the US served more than **145,000 free meals** in two months.
