**Hospitality** is often called the most important Kazakh tradition. A guest, even an unexpected one, should be welcomed and fed. Visiting a Kazakh home, it helps to know a few rules, and the most noticeable one involves **tea** (*shai*).

## The half-full bowl

Tea is served in small, wide bowls called **kese**, often with milk. A good host pours only **part-way**, around a third to half full, and keeps **refilling** it throughout the conversation. This is respectful in two ways: the tea stays **hot**, and the host shows they are happy to keep serving you, and that you’re welcome to **stay and talk**.

A bowl filled **to the brim** is traditionally read the other way: drink up, it’s time to go. In modern families this is rarely meant literally, but many Kazakhs know the saying.

**Useful tip:** when you’ve had enough, thank your host and say so politely; otherwise the refills will keep coming.

## Where to sit

The place of honour, the **tor**, is the seat **farthest from the door**. It goes to elders or honoured guests. If you are offered it, it’s polite to hesitate, and then accept.

## The dastarkhan

The meal itself is laid out as a **dastarkhan**: a spread of bread, sweets, nuts, fruit and dishes, often far more than guests could eat. Its abundance is a statement of generosity and family honour, and bread is treated with special respect: it should never be thrown away or placed upside down.

Small customs like these make the difference between being a visitor and being a welcome guest.
