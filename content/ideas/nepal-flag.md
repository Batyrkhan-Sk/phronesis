Almost every national flag is a rectangle. **Nepal’s** is two crimson **pennants** stacked on top of each other, edged in blue, with a white **moon** in the upper one and a white **sun** in the lower. It’s the only national flag that isn’t a quadrilateral, and the only one **taller than it is wide**.

## A flag you construct

When the modern flag was adopted on **16 December 1962**, the constitution didn’t just describe it. It gave a **step-by-step geometric construction**: draw a line of a given length, measure along it, join these points, draw an arc from here. It reads like a school geometry problem, and mathematicians and programmers have enjoyed following it ever since.

Follow the steps and you get a shape whose **width-to-height ratio is irrational**: roughly **1:1.219**. No simple fraction describes it.

## What it means

The double pennant is often linked to the Himalayas, or to the two main religions, **Hinduism and Buddhism**. A popular reading says the moon stands for peace and the sun for hard work, and that together they express the hope that Nepal will last **as long as the sun and moon**. These are interpretations rather than official definitions.
