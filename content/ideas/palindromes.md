Language has *racecar*, *level* and the most famous of all, **“A man, a plan, a canal – Panama!”**, credited to the British puzzler Leigh Mercer.

Numbers have them too (12321). The mathematical question of how often numbers become palindromic when you add them to their own reversal still has open cases: nobody has proved whether **196** ever becomes one.

## The biological version

DNA has two strands that pair A–T and G–C. A sequence is a **biological palindrome** when one strand read forwards equals the other strand read backwards:

```
5′ – G A A T T C – 3′
3′ – C T T A A G – 5′
```

Read either strand from its 5′ end and you get **GAATTC**.

Bacteria make **restriction enzymes** that recognise sequences like this and cut DNA there. It works as an immune system against viruses. In the 1970s scientists began using these enzymes as **molecular scissors**, which launched genetic engineering. The enzyme that cuts GAATTC, EcoRI, is still used in labs every day.

The same symmetry that makes a sentence fun to read is something molecules can recognise.
