Some films contain a scream you have almost certainly heard before. A character falls, is thrown or is shot, and out comes a short, rough yell that is the same in hundreds of films. It is called the **Wilhelm scream**.

## Where it came from

The sound was recorded for **Distant Drums** (1951), a Warner Bros. western. In one scene a soldier wading through the Everglades is grabbed by an alligator. The scream was added in post-production and kept in the studio’s sound library, where it was reused.

## A name from another film

In **The Charge at Feather River** (1953), a character called **Private Wilhelm** is hit by an arrow and lets out the same cry. Years later the sound designer **Ben Burtt**, then a student, noticed it, gave it that name, and began slipping it into the films he worked on, including **Star Wars**. Others followed, and it became a tradition: hiding the scream is a small wink among sound editors.

## Whose voice?

The voice is usually credited to **Sheb Wooley**, a singer and character actor who had a small role in *Distant Drums*. The case rests on a studio memo listing actors booked for extra vocal recording. Wooley died in 2003 without confirming it, so treat it as likely rather than proven.

## Why it works as conversation

It costs nothing to try. Mention it before a film and your friends will be listening for it all evening. It is also a neat reminder that the soundtrack is built, not just recorded: some of the sounds you remember best were borrowed.
