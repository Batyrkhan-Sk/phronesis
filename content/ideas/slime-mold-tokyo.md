*Physarum polycephalum* is a bright yellow slime mould: a **single cell** that can grow to the size of a dinner plate. It has no brain and no neurons. It still solves problems.

## The experiment

Researchers led by **Atsushi Tero and Toshiyuki Nakagaki** placed oat flakes on a dish at the positions of **36 cities around Tokyo**, put the slime mould where Tokyo is, and let it grow. Bright light, which the mould avoids, stood in for mountains and the sea.

At first it spread everywhere. Then it **pruned** weak tubes and **thickened** the useful ones. After about a day it had produced a network of tubes linking the food sources. In **efficiency, cost and resilience to broken links** it was comparable to the real **Tokyo-area rail network**.

## How a cell can do this

The rule is simple: tubes carrying **more flow grow thicker**, and tubes carrying **less flow shrink**. Applied everywhere at once, this local feedback produces good global solutions. The team turned it into a mathematical model that engineers can use to design adaptive networks.

Earlier, Nakagaki had shown the mould could find the **shortest path through a maze**. The research won **Ig Nobel Prizes** in 2008 and 2010, awarded for work that first makes people laugh and then makes them think.
