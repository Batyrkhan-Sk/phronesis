Imagine you read this sentence: “Science is just a theory, so schools should teach everything else too, including that the Earth is flat.” Is it a sincere view, or a joke at its expense? On a page, with no face or voice attached, you often can’t tell.

That is the heart of **Poe’s law**.

## Where it comes from

In August 2005, in a discussion about creationism on the Christian Forums website, another user had remarked that a joke post needed a “winky” to be recognised as one. A user called **Nathan Poe** replied that without a winking smiley or some other blatant display of humour, it is “utterly impossible” to parody a creationist in a way that someone won’t mistake for the genuine article.

The point was not really about creationism. It was that some sincere views are so extreme that a parody has to be even more extreme to be funny, and by then it has often reached a place where real people already are.

It is not named after Edgar Allan Poe. The name belongs to the forum user, whose real identity is not part of the story.

## Older than the name

The problem is much older than 2005. In 1983 a Usenet etiquette guide by Jerry Schwarz already advised people to avoid sarcasm because, without **voice inflection and body language**, it is easily misread. Poe’s law is the same insight, applied to a particular case and given a catchy name.

## Why it works both ways

People usually describe the law as “satire gets mistaken for sincerity”. The reverse also happens: a sincere extreme view gets shrugged off as a joke. Either way the reader is missing the same thing, a clear signal of **intent**.

## Using it kindly

The most useful thing about Poe’s law is what it suggests about your own behaviour:

- Before you decide someone is a fanatic, remember they might be joking.
- Before you decide someone is joking, remember they might not be.
- If you are the one being ironic in writing, add a signal. A smiley is not cowardice; it is how a joke survives the trip.

It is a good law to bring up when a screenshot is going round and everyone is arguing about whether the author meant it.
