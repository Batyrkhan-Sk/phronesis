“The only man-made structure visible from the Moon” is one of the most repeated facts in the world, and one of the most easily checked.

## Older than spaceflight

In **1754**, the English antiquary **William Stukeley**, writing about Hadrian’s Wall, said it was surpassed only by the Chinese Wall, which “may be discerned at the Moon”. Nobody could check. The claim reappeared in **Ripley’s Believe It or Not!** in **1932** and in textbooks for decades.

## The arithmetic

The wall is long, more than **21,000 km** counting every section in a 2012 survey, but it is only a few metres **wide**, and often the same colour as the land around it. From the Moon, about 384,000 km away, it would look like a **human hair seen from 3 km**.

## From orbit

From **low Earth orbit**, 160–320 km up, NASA says the wall is “**barely visible**, and only under nearly perfect conditions”, no more conspicuous than many other human-made things. Highways, airports and city lights at night are far easier to see.

In **2003**, **Yang Liwei**, China’s first astronaut, said plainly that he had **not** been able to see it. Many Chinese textbooks that repeated the claim were later revised.

**A useful takeaway:** a “fact” that is impressive, easy to repeat and hard to check can last centuries.
