In autumn **2020**, **Nathan Apodaca** of Idaho Falls found that his truck wouldn’t start, so he took his **skateboard** to work. On the way he filmed himself rolling along the road, sipping **Ocean Spray** cran-raspberry juice and mouthing along to Fleetwood Mac’s **“Dreams”**.

It was calm and happy and lasted under 30 seconds. It was also exactly what people wanted in the middle of a pandemic. The video passed **50 million views**.

## What happened next

- **“Dreams”** (1977) re-entered the **Billboard Hot 100** at **No. 21** on 17 October 2020, then rose to **No. 12**: Fleetwood Mac’s highest placing in **32 years**.
- It went to No. 1 on Billboard’s rock digital song sales chart and into the top 50 streamed songs on Spotify and Apple Music in several countries.
- **Ocean Spray** gave Apodaca a **new pickup truck**.
- Band members **Mick Fleetwood**, **Lindsey Buckingham** and **Stevie Nicks** posted their own TikTok responses.

## Why old songs go viral

TikTok works through **sounds**: a short audio clip that millions of people reuse in their own videos. A song from 1977 competes on equal terms with one from last week. For older listeners it brings back memories; for teenagers it’s simply new.

Since then, record labels have treated TikTok as a way to **rediscover their back catalogues**, and songs decades old have re-entered the charts after going viral. Musically it’s the same pattern Laver described for clothes: once something is old enough to have lost its associations, it can feel fresh again.
