**Otrar** stood on the Syr Darya river in what is now southern **Kazakhstan**, a rich trading city on the Silk Road. In the early 13th century it belonged to the **Khwarazmian Empire**, one of the strongest powers in the Muslim world.

## A caravan

By 1218 **Genghis Khan** had united the Mongols and conquered northern China. Toward Khwarazm, at first, he proposed **trade**. A caravan of around **450 merchants**, with an envoy, travelled west with goods.

At Otrar, the governor **Inalchuq** accused them of being **spies**, which some of them may well have been, as caravans gathered information. With the consent of **Sultan Muhammad II**, he had the entire caravan **executed** and seized its goods.

## The insult

Genghis Khan sent envoys to demand that Inalchuq be handed over. The sultan **beheaded** the lead envoy and **shaved the beards** of the others before sending them back, a grave humiliation.

## The consequence

In **1219** the Mongols invaded. They besieged **Otrar for five months**, took it, and executed Inalchuq. Then they swept through **Bukhara, Samarkand, Urgench** and beyond. Within a few years the Khwarazmian Empire was destroyed, its cities depopulated, and Central Asia changed for centuries.

One governor’s decision in one city set it in motion. It is one of history’s starkest examples of how a single insult can change the course of an empire.

## Afterlife

Otrar recovered partly and later declined. In **February 1405**, the conqueror **Timur** (Tamerlane) died there while preparing an invasion of China. Today its ruins are an archaeological site near the city of Shymkent.
