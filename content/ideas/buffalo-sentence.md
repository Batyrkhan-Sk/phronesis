There are three ingredients:

1. **Buffalo**: the city in New York state (used as an adjective, “from Buffalo”)
2. **buffalo**: the animal (plural, the same as singular)
3. **buffalo**: a verb meaning to bully or intimidate

Now read it with the hidden words added back:

> **(Buffalo buffalo)** *[that]* **Buffalo buffalo buffalo** — **buffalo** — **Buffalo buffalo.**

> *Bison from Buffalo, [that] bison from Buffalo bully, themselves bully bison from Buffalo.*

The trick relies on two features of English: nouns can be **zero-plural** (one buffalo, two buffalo), and the relative pronoun “that” can be **dropped** (“the cake [that] I ate”). Remove the signposts and the sentence becomes a puzzle.

Linguists use it to show that grammar is **structure, not just word order**. Your brain normally builds the structure so fast you never notice. You only notice when it has to work as hard as it does here.
