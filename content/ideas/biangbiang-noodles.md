In Xi’an, cooks stretch dough into **long, broad belts**, sometimes as wide as a belt, and slap them against the counter as they pull. The noodles are boiled and topped with chilli flakes, garlic and spring onion, then doused with **sizzling oil**. They come from **Shaanxi** cuisine and were once a filling, quick meal for workers.

## The character

The name is written with a single character for *biáng*, repeated twice. In its traditional form it has **58 strokes**, or **42** in simplified Chinese, and variants run from 56 to 71. It is one of the most complex characters in modern use. It contains, among other parts, the characters for *speech*, *horse*, *long*, *moon*, *heart* and *knife*.

It was **not in the Kangxi dictionary** or in modern dictionaries. For years, menus and signs printed it as a picture, or wrote the name in pinyin. Only on **20 March 2020** did **Unicode 13.0** add it (U+30EDE traditional, U+30EDD simplified), so it could finally be typed.

## How people remember it

Locals learn it through a **rhyme** that walks through the strokes:

> A dot rises to the sky, / Over two bends by the Yellow River’s side. / An eight opens wide, / Speech enters inside…

The name *biáng* is most likely **onomatopoeia**: the slap of the dough hitting the table.
