Picture Italian food without tomato sauce, Irish food without potatoes, Thai or Sichuan food without chillies, or Swiss chocolate. Before **1492**, none of those were possible. Tomatoes, chillies, potatoes, maize, vanilla and cacao all come from the **Americas**.

The historian **Alfred Crosby** called the huge transfer of plants, animals and diseases between the hemispheres the **Columbian exchange**.

## Italy and the suspicious tomato

The earliest Italian record of tomatoes is from **1548**, when a basket arrived at the Medici household in Florence. But the plant is a relative of **deadly nightshade**, and many Europeans assumed it was poisonous. In Florence it was mainly a **table decoration** until the late 17th century. The first cookbook with tomato recipes was printed in Naples in **1692**. Pairing tomato sauce with pasta came later still.

## Chillies conquer Asia

Chillies spread astonishingly fast. Portuguese and Spanish traders carried them to India, Southeast Asia and China during the **1500s**. Within a few generations they were central to cuisines from Goa to Sichuan to Thailand.

Asian food wasn’t bland before that. **Black pepper**, native to India, plus ginger, galangal and Sichuan pepper all provided heat. On History Stack Exchange, people asking whether “all spicy food is from Latin America” are usually told exactly this: the chilli is American, but the love of heat is much older.

**A useful takeaway:** “traditional” and “ancient” are not the same thing. Many national dishes are only a few centuries old.
