The statue is everywhere: a bald, smiling man with a **huge belly**, often carrying a sack, sometimes surrounded by children. Many people outside Asia call him “the **Buddha**” or “the Fat Buddha”.

## Two different figures

- **Siddhartha Gautama**, the historical **Buddha** and founder of Buddhism, lived in northern India around the 5th century BC. He is traditionally shown **slim**, often seated in meditation, with a calm expression.
- **Budai** was a **Chinese monk** of the **10th century**, whose real name is recorded as **Qieci**. *Budai* means “**cloth sack**”: he is described as wandering with a bag, and remembered for his good humour.

## From monk to future Buddha

After his death, Budai came to be venerated in **Chan Buddhism** as a manifestation of **Maitreya**, the Buddha who is expected to come in the future. His belly and laugh came to stand for **contentment**, generosity and good fortune. Some people rub the belly for luck.

## In Japan

When Chan spread to Japan as **Zen**, Budai became **Hotei**, one of the **Seven Lucky Gods** (*Shichifukujin*), who are popular around New Year.

So in many Chinese restaurants it isn’t the founder of Buddhism greeting you. It’s a cheerful wandering monk.
