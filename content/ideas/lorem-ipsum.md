The original line in Cicero reads:

> *Neque porro quisquam est, qui **dolorem ipsum** quia **dolor sit amet**, consectetur, adipisci velit…*
> “Nor is there anyone who loves pain itself, who seeks it and wants to have it, simply because it is pain…”

Placeholder text starts **mid-word**: “lorem” is the end of *dolorem*. Words were then cut, shuffled and invented. Placeholder text is meant to *look* like language without being read, so that clients judge the layout and not the words.

## Who found it

**Richard McClintock**, a Latin scholar at Hampden-Sydney College in Virginia, noticed the rare word *consectetur* and went looking. He found the Cicero passage.

The often-repeated claim that printers have used it **“since the 1500s”** is not well documented. What is clear is that **Letraset** transfer sheets spread it in the 1960s, and desktop publishing software in the 1980s made it ubiquitous.

There is an irony in it: millions of mock-ups that ask you not to read the text are quoting a philosophical argument about avoiding pain.
