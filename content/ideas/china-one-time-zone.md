China is about as wide as the continental United States, which uses four main time zones. China uses **one**: **Beijing Time**, UTC+8.

## Five zones, then one

The Republic of China defined **five** time zones, from Kunlun (UTC+5:30) in the far west to Changpai (UTC+8:30) in the northeast. After the founding of the People’s Republic in **1949**, the whole country moved to **Beijing Time**: a statement of national unity, written into the clock.

## Life in the far west

In **Xinjiang**, more than 3,000 km west of Beijing, the result is strange. In winter, in westernmost **Akto County**, the sun rises at about **10:16 a.m.** by the official clock.

So people adapt. Many in Xinjiang, especially Uyghurs, use an unofficial **Xinjiang Time**, two hours behind Beijing. Shops and offices often open from **10:00 to 19:00 Beijing Time**, which is 8 to 5 local time. When arranging to meet, people often ask: “Beijing time or Xinjiang time?”

## Why it’s interesting

Time zones feel like geography, but they are **political choices**. France spreads across twelve; Kiribati moved the date line; China, nearly as wide as the US, chose one, and the sun keeps its own schedule regardless.
